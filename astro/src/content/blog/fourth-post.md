I couldn't help myself when I saw the 6.3-megapixel plastic DSLR from the year I graduated high school listed for $25 AUD. It had no lens or CF card and although the seller claimed it was in working order I had some skepticism.

I forgot to check the CF pins when examining it. It was a bit to juggle taking the lens off of my 1100D to try it on the 300D. When I got home and remembered to check, I found some were bent. I set to straightening them with a long tweezers which is hard to do in the narrow slot. I think I broke off a corner pin that was bent over quite far. According to Gemini it may be a ground pin and since there are multiple maybe it's not needed, especially with a CF adapter I was planning on using which may share ground contacts.

#### The 300d alongside the 6d for comparison (Taken with an Iphone 8 Plus)

![Stacked astro photo](https://hobbitronics.github.io/images/300d6d.jpg)

After receiving a CF to SD card adapter in the mail, I tested it out with a 2GB SD card and it worked just fine. I can transfer photos over USB 1.1. Since I plan to shoot JPEG which are only a few MB it's tolerable. The adapter also supposedly has WIFI, but I could not find the network so either the camera has too much shielding for the signal to get out, the WIFI doesn't work or its not getting enough power from the weak camera battery.

Half press of the shutter button wasn't working until I exercised it was a bit of rubbing alcohol.

Besides the battery being on its last legs everything else seemed in good order.

### Shooting:

- I used my EF-S 18-55mm IS lens. It seems like a good pairing and produced sharp images.

- The viewfinder is a bit small, but clear and although not a pentaprism it's bright enough. All the optics on my example were quite clean compared to my 1100D so it's actually significantly better to look through.

- The camera is comfortable to hold. The grip is thicker than on the 1100d. The plastic body feels fine, and has held up well over the years.

- Everything is a bit slow, including booting. Once the camera is on taking a single photo is normal, but the rate is limited to 2.5 frame/s, up to 4 frames. After that you see "busy" in the monochrome LCD status panel.

- The small 1.8 inch lcd is only enough to get an impression of what you just shot. It isn't very bright and makes changing setting difficult in bright conditions.

- It kind of feels like shooting film, which I found enjoyable.

- The photos have a nice pop of color although this is a CMOS crop sensor, not CCD.

- 6.3 megapixel isn't as bad as it sounds. 12 megapixel is only 1.39X the detail.

- Low light is where things fall apart, but it does have a built in flash.

### Should you buy it?

- For 25 AUD assuming it works and you aren't a hoarder, yes.

- Remember, batteries, cards and lenses will cost money.

- Remember to check the CF pins!

- Maybe consider buying a different retro DSLR like the EOS 10D or the Pentax K100. It will feel more premium.

### How does it compare to the Iphone?

- It's better
